# Discovery Plan

## Objective

Understand how Property Management work is actually performed today, where work gets stuck, and which problems are process problems versus tooling problems.

## Stakeholders

### Customer Success Lead

Questions:

- What are the most frequent investor, tenant, and property management requests?
- Which requests create the most dissatisfaction or churn risk?
- What signals indicate urgency?
- What communication gaps create follow-up work?

Validates:

- Contact reasons
- Satisfaction risks
- Response quality
- Service expectations

### Operations / Property Management Lead

Questions:

- What happens from incident intake to resolution?
- Who owns each step?
- Where are handoffs slow or unclear?
- What categories and priorities are used today?
- What makes a ticket ready to close?

Validates:

- Real workflow
- Handoffs
- Bottlenecks
- Ownership model
- Operational rules

### Sales Team Lead

Questions:

- What expectations are sold to investors?
- Which promises are hardest to fulfil operationally?
- What information would help convert or retain investors?

Validates:

- Promise versus delivery gap
- Objections
- Differentiation opportunities

### Rent Collections / Settlement Ops

Questions:

- How are rent due dates, payment status, late payment follow-up, reconciliation, and investor communication handled?
- Where is manual work highest?
- Which payment issues create the most investor anxiety?

Validates:

- Payment visibility
- Late rent workflow
- Reconciliation effort
- Reporting dependencies

## Mapping Technique: Service Blueprint

Incident management is not only a user journey. It is a service blueprint because success depends on coordination between visible user interactions, internal actions, systems, and data.

### Blueprint Layers

- User interaction: investor or tenant reports an issue.
- Visible action: Customer Success responds and sets expectations.
- Internal action: Property Manager validates, coordinates provider, reviews cost, and follows up.
- Systems: app, email, WhatsApp, CRM, property database, contract database.
- Data: person, role, property, contract, ticket, category, SLA, status, owner.
- Evidence: user-facing history, status, next step, and timestamps.

## Process Versus Tool Diagnosis

### Process Problem

Recognised by:

- No clear owner
- No priority rule
- No SLA
- No escalation path
- No definition of done

Example:

An incident arrives and nobody knows who should own it, what needs to be asked, when to respond, or how to close it.

### Tool Problem

Recognised by:

- The process is clear but execution is fragmented.
- Information lives in email, WhatsApp, CRM, spreadsheets, Drive folders, and manual notes.
- There is no traceability or shared state.

Example:

Photos arrive through WhatsApp, contract data is in Drive, ticket status is in a spreadsheet, and the response is sent by email.
